# The first levels, played through (Phase 7 round 9 x1: level 1; round 10 y1 and round 11 z1: level 2; round 11 z3: levels 3 and 4)

Level 1 of Turok 2, the Port of Adia, played from a new game to its end on the native port
(Linux, headless): every sub-level from 51 to 59, both mission objectives (the three distress
beacons and the four children), the two portal switches, the exit warp 7100 to the Harbor Totem
(level 109), the totem defense, and the warp back to the hub (level 60). The script is
reproducible: one `--input @FILE` and one `T2_TELEPORT` list, generated by
`tools/port/playthrough.py`. Teleports only put the player next to each progression point,
facing it. From there the game's own logic does each step: the pickup, the switch, the cage
door, the child, the beacon, the objective counters and messages, and the warp.

with a key byte (0x0F) decrements that link group, and the group's records fire when it reaches 0
("all distress beacons activated. mission complete.").

## 4. The totem defense (level 109)

`TotemDefense_InitFight`/`_Init` start a 120 s defense (`m_TotemMissionDuration`): kill 8
enemies (`m_EnemiesToKillToSaveTotem`) before the time runs out. Four generators (actors 14, 19,
20, 21, type 1604) on the walls start when their gates (actors 1-4) open, at most 3 spawns alive
at a time (`CInstancePool__Spawn`). The state log prints a `totem:` line (state, time left,
kills, the totem's health).

- Before X1-7 the spawns never moved: no kills, the time ran out, `TotemDefense_Fail`, and after
  75 frames `TotemDefense_Update` warped to 1999: the hub.
- After X1-7/X1-8 the enemies come over the walls, attack the totem and Turok and die: with the
  pistol (`T2_WEAPON=4`), `T2_FACE_NEAREST=4000` and a tap of Z every 15 frames, 6 of 8 kills in
  120 s (the time runs out at 6/8: `TotemDefense_Fail`, then the hub); with the plasma rifle
  (`T2_WEAPON=10`): from `T2_WARP_TO=-110` 10/8 kills, saved with 23 s left; in the full
  scripted run (`--fight 10`) 8/8 with 3 s left (Turok down to 36 health). Then
  `TotemDefense_Complete` ("MISSION COMPLETE", the totem's rest animation,
  `m_bLevel1TotemFlags`, cheat bit 0x8000), the helpers stop, and 90 frames later the warp to 1999,
  the hub.
- Attract demo 3 plays in this level (20,946-22,148 of the boot loop): its Raptoids now come in and
  hit Turok too (health 99 at 21,600); the boot loop's mode switches are unchanged (25,778 the
  title again).

How the records work (`CScene__GetInstanceEventRecords`, `CScene__GetRegionEventRecords`): the
first table of a level's links block is sorted by the **owning actor index** (`levels.py`'s
`id`): an actor's records fire when it fires its triggers (mask 1: its death, 0x4000: a door
opening, 0x80000: an action object "go"); the second table is sorted by **region info index**:
the player entering a region of that info fires them (mask 1, `CPlayer` move,
func_0024C47C). The record's key byte is the link group it counts down (the doors that open
after N deaths). The graveyards wake their zombies by region (133: info 4 near door 27, info 7
the sister's hall; 135: info 8 opens door 59, info 9 "graveyard located." and the generators).

### 6.4 New debug switches (DEBUG-ONLY, port/game/t2debug.c)

- `T2_AIM_AT=F1-F2:x,y,z[;...]`: from F1 to F2 turn the player (yaw and camera pitch) at a
  point: the soul gates (not actors the aim assists know).
- `T2_AIM_ENEMY=F1-F2[,R][;...]`: `T2_AIM_NODE=-1` (the nearest live actor, yaw and pitch) only in
  those windows, so the walks between fights keep their facing. The flying sisters need the pitch.
- `T2_TELEPORT=F:x,y,z[,rotY]!`: a `!` also fires the region's "entered" triggers
  (`CGameRegion__FireTriggers(region, 1)`, what the player's move does): the graveyards' zombies
  only wake when the player walks into their halls.
- `T2_EVENT_LOG`: `objectives A/B` is now the counters of the current level's hub (`T2Port_HubLevelOf`).
- Round 11 (z1): `T2_AIM_ENEMY=F1-F2,R,A`: level actor A first (the nearest live actor when A is
  dead or out of range; `T2_AIM_NODE=actor:N[,R]` underneath); `T2_FACE_PITCH=1`: `T2_FACE_NEAREST`
  also sets the camera pitch (the portable atan2f of t2bosslog.c).

## 17. Fewer aids in levels 1 and 2 (Phase 7 round 14, part c5)

### 17.1 Aiming with the stick only (`playthrough.py`: `face_yaw`, `stick_turn`, `stick_aim`)

The stick's turn rate was measured on Linux (`T2_STATE_LOG=2`, level 51, extra `SX=` inputs). The
rate is the same on every frame from the first one, with no ramp-up:

| input | stick read | turn per frame |
|---|---|---|
| `SX=127` | 80 (the cap) | 0.0685 rad |
| `SX=40` | 37 | 0.00675 rad |

So SX=V reads V - 3, and the rate goes with the cube of the reading: 0.0685 * (r / 80)^3.
Positive SX makes rotY grow. The game's forward vector is (-sin rotY, -cos rotY).

`stick_aim(x, z, ry, target, f)` turns the player from the route step's teleport point and yaw
towards a point. It uses full-stick frames, then one frame at a partial reading for the rest. The
pitch is left to the game. A route step can now say `"stick": (x, y, z)` in place of
`"aim": (x, y, z)`. Both `build_level2` and `fullrun.steps` turn it into `SX` inputs. No game
state is touched.

**The two soul gates (levels 129 and 132) now use it**: no `T2_AIM_AT` in level 2. The inputs are
`SX=-54` for one frame (gate 1: 0.018 rad) and `SX=-127` for 10 frames, then `SX=-45` (gate 2:
0.695 rad). In the `--all` run (Linux Debug, `--talismans 2 --cheats-off 1,2 --cheats-on 110`),
"soul gate destroyed." comes at **35,404 and 62,204, the same frames as with `T2_AIM_AT`**. "all
soul gates destroyed." comes at 76,974, and level 2 ends with the Harbor and City totems won
(progress `totems 11000` at 90,567).

### 21.1 What the Primagen's search sees

The wait that never ended is `Primagen_UpdateMode19` (AI mode 25) in state 3: at stages 2 and 3
he flies home (`gLevelScript.home`, his spawn point (0, 588.8, 2,918.4); he hovers at y 937 and
steps round it at z 2,906-2,923) and waits there until `AI_UpdateTargets` returns a sight target
**and** he is within 50 of home. He is the level's only kind-1 actor (`m_PathFindCount` 0, the
cursor 0 of 1), so `AI_GetSightTargetInfo` runs `AI_FindBestTarget` for him every frame.

With the search instrumented (temporary prints, not committed), the only test that fails is the
line of sight. There is no view cone (his intelligence's `m_dwTypeFlags` is 4, no 0x8000), and
the range does not matter. The 0x8000 that Mode19 sets in `D_800F1F50` around its search is his own
movement collision info: the module's AI mode table gives modes 18 and 19 the collision info
`D_800F1F50`, and `Sweep_FollowGround` reads that bit. The search's own collision info is
`gCollisionInfoLineOfSight`. The line from the player's eye (y +92.2: the player intelligence's
`m_CollisionHeight`, eye offset 0) to his eye (937 + 490.5 + 169.9 = 1,597.4) hits a static model,
the lair's back structure (object 1911, bounds x -920..916, y -104..1,736, z 2,352..4,384). From
(450, 0, 2,186) it hits the front face at (342.9, 450.7, 2,361.7). He waits in that structure's
alcove. A frame of the player's view from there shows the wall and not him. No N64-offset view,
no s32 truncation and no uninitialised field was found on the way.

**Why c3's probe saw him.** `T2_BOSS_FRONT`'s probe and scan run at the start of the host frame.
The visible grid sections are released at the end of each frame (`CScene__ReleaseSectors`) and
loaded again by the next draw (`CScene__LoadVisibleGridSections`), so at that point
`gScene.m_nSectors` is 0. `Collision_TestModels` then tests no static model at all, and every
line of sight passes. At the time of the search itself, 4 sections are loaded and object 1911 is
hit. A floor scan run at the search's time (him at z 2,922.9, the player moved to each spot):
**no solid floor spot from z -1,500 to 2,300 sees him**. The only spots that do are over the pits
at the arena's front corners. A player placed there (`T2_BOSS_FRONT=800,600,0.82`) falls, and
after 3 lives the game is over.

**b2's restore is the trigger, not the cause.** `--level 6` with the restores switched off (a
test switch, not committed) kills him. Skipping one of 114's four "force field down" records
(index 27), or the two other restored records (33, 35), changes nothing in the lair; skipping all
four "force field down" records kills him. Those records put 114's force field action
(actor 68) back in mode 1 when 114 is entered again after the Mother. Both record walkers do this,
the second only for kinds 1, 2, 4 and 10, in the N64's order. The rest of the run then differs,
including where he hovers and where the player stands when each wait starts, and the sight line is
a knife edge. In a run that worked, the stage-3 wait ended with the player at (450.3, 0, 1,795.5)
and him at (0.3, 937, 2,920.5). In a stuck run, with the player at (450.1, 0, 1,781.3) and him at

## 24. Aim assist off: closed-loop stick aiming in levels 1-4 (Phase 7 round 15, part d3)

### 24.1 The mechanism (`T2_AIM_STICK`, `T2_AIM_FIFO`, `tools/port/aimdrive.py`)

The aim assists (`T2_FACE_NEAREST`, `T2_AIM_AT`, `T2_AIM_ENEMY`, `T2_AIM_NODE`) write the
player's yaw and the camera pitch every frame. Round 14's `stick_aim` (17.1) only turns towards
a fixed point, open loop. This round adds a closed loop:

- `T2_AIM_STICK=F1-F2[;...]` (schedule frames, `port/game/t2debug.c`). Inside a window the assists
  write nothing. They still pick their target with the same rules: nearest, sticky, level actor
  first, the boss phase's node. Then they report its point (`T2Port_AimReport`). `T2_BOSS_FRONT`
  is not changed: it is not used in levels 1-4.
- `T2_AIM_FIFO=DIR` (`port/app/game.cpp`). After each frame in which a target was reported, the
  game writes one line to the fifo `DIR/out`:
  `aim F px py pz rotY ex ey ez pitch tx ty tz`. These are the player position and yaw, the
  camera eye and pitch, and the target point, as exact `%a` hex floats. The game then blocks on
  one line from `DIR/in`: `SX SY`, the stick of port 1 for the next frame (over the script's).
  It is input only: nothing in the game state is written. Outside the windows nothing is
  exchanged, and the build without these variables is unchanged: the default `--all` run's det
  log is byte-identical to 8b42da15's (580 lines).
- `tools/port/aimdrive.py [--simple] [--stats FILE] -- turok2 ...` makes the two fifos, starts
  the game with `T2_AIM_FIFO`, and answers each line from that line alone. It is deterministic:
  two runs give identical logs.
- `playthrough.py OUT --all ... --stick-aim 1,2,3,4 [--assist-keep 1,110]` (`fullrun.py`) writes
  `T2_AIM_STICK` with the frame ranges of those levels, minus the fights in `--assist-keep`
  (`1`: the Blind One, `110`: the City Totem). Run the game under the driver:
  `runfull.sh`-style `python3 tools/port/aimdrive.py --stats RUN/stats.txt -- build/app/turok2 ...`
  with the usual `teleport.env`.
- `tools/port/aimstats.py LOG [LOG2 ...]` compares runs: damage, hits, lowest health and deaths per
  fight level, the totems' kills and time left, and the frame each totem and boss bit was set.
  `aimstats.py --stick STATS` shows the tracking per aim window: mean yaw and pitch error, and the
  share of frames within 0.05 rad of the target.

### 24.2 The stick, measured (`aimdrive.py --probe SX,SY[,N]`, level 51, Linux Release)

- The game reads the pad once per tick: every second host frame. A stick change shows up in the
  pose 4 host frames later.
- **Stick X turns at a rate.** Re-measured, the cube law of 17.1 is exact: SX=8, 13, 20, 30 and 45
  turn 0.000017, 0.000134, 0.000660, 0.002641 and 0.009944 rad per host frame. The model
  0.0685 * ((SX - 3) / 80)^3 gives 0.000017, 0.000134, 0.000657, 0.002633 and 0.009912.
- **Stick Y does not turn the pitch at a rate.** It sets a look angle,
  -(reading / 80) * (pi / 2), where + SY looks down. The camera pitch eases towards that angle
  by 5.41 % of the difference per tick, and back towards 0 when the stick is released. Examples:
  SY=127 gives -pi/2, SY=40 gives -0.726 and SY=-60 gives +1.118. The pitch first changes by
  0.0848, 0.0392 and 0.0604 on a tick. Releasing at -0.67 brings it back to -0.14 in 56 frames.
- The controller turns by GAIN (0.8) of the predicted error per tick. It is a Smith predictor:
  the answers still in flight (the last 3) are applied to the pose first. It adds the target's
  own bearing and elevation rate while the target stays the same. Stick X is the inverse of the
  cube law; stick Y is the look angle that moves the pitch by that share. `--simple` is the
  first version: GAIN 0.35 of the observed error, no prediction. On a still point it turns
  1.2 rad and holds within 0.001 rad in about 30 frames.

### 25.1 Where the pickups are

Weapons and ammo are level actors in the grid sections (`tools/assets/levels.py`). Cart entry 2
maps an actor's object to its pickup id: `ids[object + 4]`, where the first 8 bytes are a header.
The pickup tables are in the ELF:

- `sWeaponPickups`: rows of 0x18 bytes, `{m_Id, weapon, ammo type, amount}`. The ids run from 2001
  to 2122.
- `sAmmoPickups`: rows of 0x14 bytes, `{m_Id, type, amount}`. The ids run from 2200 to 3027.

The `m_Id` is the actor's pickup id (`CPlayer__CollectPickup`). Useful ids for levels 1-3:

| id | gives |
|---|---|
| 2004 | the pistol (4) +20 bullets |
| 2005 | the mag 60 (5) |
| 2008 | the shotgun (8) +10 shells |
| 2010 | the plasma rifle (10) +100 |
| 2016 | the grenade launcher (15) +5 |
| 2017 | the scorpion launcher (16) +3 |
| 2500 / 2504 | bullets x10 / x40 (type 2, at most 50) |
| 2501 / 2502 | shells x3 / x10 (type 6, at most 20) |
| 2503 | explosive shells x3 (type 7, at most 10) |
| 2200 / 2201 | arrows x5 / x10 |
| 2202 / 2203 | tek arrows x3 / x5 |
| 2601 | plasma cells x150 |

Ammo is taken without its weapon. A full type is not taken. A new game starts with the talon (0)
and the bow (2). `Pickup_GiveWeapon` selects a new weapon when its index is higher than the
current one's. When a weapon runs dry the game falls back to another one with ammo, down to the
talon.

### 25.2 Changing weapons through the input stream

The default control style is the stick wheel (`CBarrel__RunStick`). `CBarrel__RunButtons`, the
A/B cycling, is used only with the other `m_WeaponSelect`, so a tap of A or B does nothing here
(tried: the weapon stays). The stick wheel works like this:

- Holding A (0x8000) opens `sBarrelLayoutGroup1`: talon, flare, bow, pistol, tranquilizer,
  shotgun, plasma rifle, firestorm.
- Holding B (0x4000) opens `sBarrelLayoutGroup0`: sunfire pod, cerebral bore, PFM layer, grenade
  launcher, scorpion launcher, flame thrower, razor wind, nuke.
- The analog stick picks one of the 8 slots (`CBarrel__GetStickDirection`: 0 up, then clockwise).
  The slot's weapon is requested if it has ammo.

`fullrun.wheel(weapon, f)` writes these inputs: the button held for 30 frames, and the stick
pushed towards the slot from frame 10 to frame 25. A route step can say `{"weapon": W}`. The wheel
then runs `WHEEL_BEFORE` = 50 frames before the step's teleport, which sets the yaw again.
Checked in level 129: `A` held with `SX=-90 SY=-90` changes the grenade launcher (15) to the
shotgun (8) (`state:` `reqw 8 wheel 2/0/5`).

### 27.1 Driving the N64 without the keyboard

The emulator's keyboard path (`n64trace.py --input`, `refshots.py`) does not reach the game in this
setup: with `START@700`, `START@1500` and A taps the attract loop runs frame for frame as with no

followed the demos, so this went unnoticed. `tools/port/n64trace.py` got four switches
(one emulator frame = one game frame of Turok 2 here; the port's frames are two per game frame):

- `--start-warp ID@N`: at emulator frame N (the attract loop) write what
  `CEngineApp__StartGameAtWarp(app, ID)` writes (`m_Settings.nextMode` 13, `fadeMode` 2,

### 27.4 The camera shake's random numbers (a host bug, fixed)

With the RNG set to the same value on both sides at the lair's first game frames (`T2_RNG_SET` /
`--poke` above), the N64 took **12 numbers per game frame** and the port **6** (the LCG stepped
back to the poked value). `n64trace.py --calls Random_Blend` names the callers: per camera (two:
0x801198F0 and 0x806FF120) three from `CCamera__UpdateTremor` and three from
`CShake__ApplyOffset`; the port's `T2_RNG_TRACE` shows only `CCamera__UpdateTremor`.
`CShake__ApplyOffset(&cam->m_Shake, cam->m_Shake.m_vPos, ...)` draws when the distance (0) is under
`m_Radius`: 1 on the N64 (`--args a0:CShake:m_Radius:f`), 0 in the port (gdb), and the port's
`m_vPos.y` was 1.0. A gdb watchpoint found the writer: `CCamera__Construct`
(src/us/code/func_0042F9B0.c) views `&view->m_Shake` as six floats at the N64 offsets (two 4-byte
list pointers, then `m_vPos` at 0x08, `m_Radius` at 0x14); on the host the pointers are 8 bytes, so
the stores hit `m_pNext` and `m_vPos.x/y`. The fix stores the members (TARGET_PORT; asm_diff 0);
`CShake__Construct` (func_00281C08.c, the same view, no C caller) is fixed the same way. After it the
port takes 12 numbers per game frame like the N64.

**It changes every random number after the first camera is built**, so the scripted whole-game route
(tuned on the old stream) no longer works: with the fix the default command wins the Harbor Totem
and ends in game over in 125 at ~155,000. **`T2_LEGACY_SHAKE=1`** (port/game/t2debug.c) makes
`CCamera__Construct` store as before; `fullrun.py` and `playthrough.py` now write it into
`teleport.env`, so their runs are unchanged. A player (no env) gets the N64's numbers. Re-tuning the
route on the N64 stream is future work.

Not fixed, for the coordinator: `CShake__Set` (func_00281C60.c, `RecordRecAt18` with `pad[8]`) and
`CShake__Advance` (func_00281CC0.c, `s32 arg0 + 0x18/0x2C/0x40`) are the same N64 layout and have no
C callers; `n64viewcheck.py` flags none of the three (a cast of `&obj->member` to a local struct
type is not one of its site kinds).

Still different after the fix (a lead, not chased): the props `lvl 10`-`17` of the lair (mode 0,
next to the spawn) are at their spawn positions with animation 4 frame 0.5 on the N64 from the first
game frame, and at a displaced position with animation 0 frame 1/3 in the port.

## 28. The stick driver picks its own target and fires: the sisters by stick (Phase 7 round 16, part e2)

### 28.1 The mechanism

Round 15's driver (section 24) turned towards the one point the assist chose (the nearest), and the
script's Z taps fired blind. This round the game reports every target the assist would accept, and the
driver chooses, leads and fires. It still sends input only.

- **Candidates** (`port/game/t2debug.c` `T2Port_AimCand`, hooks in `T2Port_FaceNearest` and
  `t2bosslog.c` `T2Port_AimNode`). Inside a `T2_AIM_STICK` window, the assists report each target that
  passes their filters: live, in range, and for a boss the phase's node box nearest the eye. The
  nearest 12 go on the `aim` line after the 13 fixed fields:
  `n` then `id x y z hp kind` each (`%a` floats), then `hp H` (the player's health). `id` is the level
  actor's index, or 10000 + the instance-pool slot. `kind 1` is the assist's own choice set. `kind 0` is
  a live enemy outside that set within 600 units: a boss phase's set or the `actor:A` of a sister fight
  leave out the enemy that is biting the player, and a player would shoot that one first.
- **Fire** (`port/app/game.cpp`). The answer to an `aim` line may be `SX SY Z`. `Z` 1 holds the Z
  button and 0 releases it, over the script's, on that frame. The script's Z taps still fire on frames
  with no target reported.
- **The driver** (`tools/port/aimdrive.py`):
  - `Picker` chooses the target. Its cost is (distance / 1,000 + 0.1) x (1 + 0.5 x the crosshair's
    angle to it) x (1 + 2 x health / 100). A `kind 0` costs 1.5 times more, and the current target
    0.75 times less. So the near and the weak go first, and among equals the one under the crosshair.
    A target whose health has not dropped for 300 frames costs 4 times more for 900 frames (`kind 0`:
    50 times more for 3,000 frames). This drops the targets the weapon does not reach.
  - The lead: each candidate's velocity comes from its last position (same id, no jump, smoothed). The
    aim point moves by velocity x distance / 150 (the shot's flight). The stick's own lag stays with
    the controller (`Aim`, the bearing rate).
  - `Trigger` fires a tap of 6 frames every 15 frames (as the script), only when the crosshair is
    within 40 units of the led point at its distance (at least 0.03 rad) in yaw, and 1.5 times that in
    pitch.
  - Tunables: `T2_AIMD_PICK=0` (the assist's target, round 15), `T2_AIMD_FIRE=0` (the script's Z),
    `T2_AIMD_WANG`, `T2_AIMD_WHP`, `T2_AIMD_WKEEP`, `T2_AIMD_THREAT`, `T2_AIMD_IMMUNE*`, `T2_AIMD_PSPEED`,
    `T2_AIMD_LEAD`, `T2_AIMD_HITR`, `T2_AIMD_FIRE_EVERY`, `T2_AIMD_FIRE_HOLD`. `fullrun.py --env K=V`
    puts one into `teleport.env`.
  - `--stats`: two more columns (the chosen id and Z), and every 50 frames a `# F hp H id:dist:hp[t]`
    line with the candidates (`t`: kind 0). `aimstats.py --stick` adds the shots and the target
    switches per window.
- The default command is unchanged: no `T2_AIM_STICK`, no candidates. Its `progress`, `event`,
  `pickup`, `hurt`, `death`, `bosstrace` and `lairpeek` lines are identical to 5d835e51's (md5
  `5ed5092b…`, END 2).

## 30. Walking instead of teleporting: the waypoint walker (Phase 7 round 16, part e3)

**The navigation mesh** (`tools/port/nav.py`, port-free: the ROM through tools/assets/levels.py). A
sub-level's floor is its collision regions: triangles with their three edge neighbours (vertex y is the
floor, w the ceiling). The player crosses an edge the way `Sweep_CrossRegionEdge` (func_0023B664.c)
lets him:

- a border edge (no neighbour) is a wall;
- a steep region (region flag 8, or its normal when flag 0x2000 is set: `CGameRegion__IsSteep`) is a
  wall when its highest corner is above his feet. So walls are regions too, and a steep region lower
  than the feet is a slide down. Steep regions with flag 0x10 are climbable (`PlayerClimb_Enter`);
- the region-flag 0x80, info 0x400000 and flags52 0x80 boundaries depend on the mover's collision flags
  and stay closed (conservative);
- deep water costs 4 times its length, a drop of more than 120 is one-way and costs 400.

A* runs over the regions (centroid to edge midpoint to centroid). The simple stupid funnel then pulls
the path through the shared edges, shrunk by 40 units at their ends: the waypoints. Static objects
are not in the mesh. Their world boxes (`CStaticObject__Construct`: bfloat16 offsets from the
position) can be routed round (`T2_NAV_BOXES=1`). They are off by default: a box is only the broad
phase. Hollow buildings, corridor pieces and floor mounds have boxes too, and going round boxes made
135's walk (12, 1) fail. That walk goes straight through when the boxes are ignored.

```sh
python3 tools/port/nav.py baserom.us.z64 134 78.7,1024,-870.5 1650,1024,-870   # the waypoints
python3 tools/port/navcands.py baserom.us.z64 RUN/run.log [--table]   # every teleport of a run, classified
python3 tools/port/navplot.py baserom.us.z64 135 STATS F1 F2 out.png [A B]   # one walk drawn (matplotlib)
```

**The walker** (`aimdrive.py`'s `WalkDrive`, input only, deterministic). It drives
`T2_WALK_PLAN="F1-F2@SUB:X,Y,Z,ROTY;..."` (each window is also a `T2_MOVE_DRIVE` window, so the
game sends its "move" line every frame). At a window's first line it plans from where the player
really stands. Then, every frame:

- the stick turns him to the next waypoint, with the answers still in flight predicted as in d3's `Aim`;
- C-up walks while the yaw error is under 0.6 rad;
- a waypoint counts as passed within 60 units; within 30 of the goal he stops and turns to ROTY;
- stuck (under 25 units in 36 frames of C-up): back off 10 frames, sidestep 24 (the side alternates),
  and plan again, with a 100-unit box where he stopped as an obstacle.

The answer is "SX 0 C" (stick X, C buttons) on the existing fifo; nothing in the game changed.
`fullrun.py --nav L,...` replaces the route teleports of `NAV_WALKS[L]` (less `NAV_SKIP`) by walk
windows of `nav_frames()` frames: the path at 0.85 of the walking speed, the two turns at the stick's
full rate, 40 more. Like d5's `--walk`, the step and the later steps of its level move by the window.
`--nav-only L:I.J,...` takes just those walks. `--walk-probe` also teleports at each window's end, so

### 31.1 One answer protocol for the input pipe

Three drivers answer the game's `aim` and `move` lines: AimDrive (round 15 d3, e2), LairDrive
(C30) and WalkDrive (e3). Before the merge each line kind had its own third field: an `aim` answer
`SX SY Z` (0/1, e2), a `move` answer `SX SY C` (a hex C mask, C30 and e3), and e5's fire gate
worked on the script's Z beside them. Now every answer is

```
SX SY [cH] [z0|z1] [r0|r1]
```

the stick (or `- -`), then tokens that each set one button group over the script's for the next
frame: `cH` the C buttons (U 8, D 4, L 2, R 1), `z` the Z trigger (fire), `r` the R trigger
(jump). A group no token names stays the script's; when an `aim` and a `move` line go out in one
frame their tokens add up. In `game.cpp`, `AimFifoButtons` parses the tokens into a mask and
values and `AimFifoApply` puts them over the pad in both pad paths (`ContRead`,
`ContReadHeadless`); `ScriptedButtonsGated` applies e5's `T2_FIRE_GATE` to the script's Z unless
the driver sets Z itself. AimDrive answers `SX SY [zN]`, LairDrive `- - cH`, WalkDrive
`SX 0 cH [r1]`. The default run and `--stick-aim 2 --assist-keep 110` give the same det lines
before and after the change.

### 32-f5.1 What the driver sees now (the `aim` line)

Two fields are appended after `hp H` (`port/game/t2debug.c` `T2Port_AimPose`); older parsers ignore them:

- `tot HP TIME KILLS NEED` while a totem defense runs: the totem's health, the seconds left, the kills
  and the kills needed (the HUD's two bars). **The totem itself is never hurt**:
  `TotemDefense_OnEvent` is empty and `TotemDefense_UpdateFight` fails the defense on time alone
  (8 kills in 120 s). Its health stays 1,280 (`5` after `>> 8`) for the whole fight.
- `lv N`: the level (the driver's per-level rules below).
- A candidate's `kind` is `2` when it is an attacker of the totem (its sight target is the totem:
  `TotemDefense_UpdateFight` sends every helper past `maxHelpers` there). Truthy like 1.

`aimdrive.py --stats` prints them in its `#` lines (`a` after an attacker, `tot 5 99s 2/8` at the end).

**A logging bug fixed (`T2Port_TotemLive`).** `gTotemDefense.obj` and `.def` are never cleared:
after the totem's level is left they point into the freed actor array and level data (the N64
too; the game never reads them there, only its own step callback does). The `totem:` state line
read them in every later level (state 1 with `totem hp 0`, `-798464` or `4236247`, different run
to run: f1's report). Both the line and the aim-line fields now need the totem to be one of
`gScene.m_pActors` still drawn by `TotemDefense_DrawTotem`; outside its level nothing is printed.
Debug code only: no game state changes.

### 32-f5.6 Determinism: run each game in its own directory
